Beat the bot

A market maker running the Avellaneda-Stoikov model, in the setting of their 2008 paper: the mid price is a random walk with σ = 2 over one session of 200 steps, and buyers and sellers arrive at rate A e-kδ (A = 140, k = 1.5) when a quote sits δ away from the mid. The bot has risk aversion γ = 0.1. It quotes around a reservation price that moves against its inventory, and its spread narrows as the session ends.

Either quote next to it and try to make more money, or trade against its quotes and try to take money from it. P&L is marked at the final mid.

You and the bot each trade with your own copy of the order flow, drawn from the same random numbers, so a quote at least as close to the mid as the bot's fills whenever the bot's does. Neither of you takes orders away from the other. ↑ ↓ change your half spread, ← → move both quotes down or up, space pauses.

Buy at the bot's ask or sell at its bid, one unit at a time. Each trade moves the bot's inventory, and its quotes move with it. b buys, s sells, space pauses. With the tip on, you see a noisy forecast of the final mid (error sd 0.5).
